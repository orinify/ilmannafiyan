# The 10 Project Management Principles That Always Deliver

After successfully leading several projects from start to finish, I’ve come to realize that there are
certain project management practices that truly stand out. These lessons aren’t just things I’ve read
in books, but they’re ideas I’ve seen work well in real life. In particular, there are 10 things from 
the book *97 Things Every Project Manager Should Know* that I strongly agree with based on my own experience. Here’s a look at these principles and why they resonate with me.

## 1. Get Users Involved As Early As Possible
One of the biggest mistakes I’ve seen is waiting too long to bring users into the process. The earlier you involve the end users, the better. When users are part of the discussion from the start, you can be sure you’re building something they actually need. It also reduces the chances of costly changes down the line. Trust me, users can help you catch issues early on that might otherwise be overlooked.

## 2. Make Project Sponsors Write Their Own Requirements
While it might seem easier to take on the task of writing requirements yourself, it’s essential to make project sponsors take responsibility for their own needs. When sponsors write their requirements, they’re more likely to understand the constraints and limitations of the project. Plus, it ensures they’re truly invested in the project’s outcome. It saves time and energy spent on back-and-forth misunderstandings.

## 3. Favor the Simple Over the Complex
Complexity is tempting, especially when we want to prove how capable we are of delivering something “next-level.” But in project management, simplicity often wins. Simple solutions are easier to maintain, easier to scale, and generally cause fewer headaches down the road. Keep things simple, and you’ll avoid unnecessary confusion.

## 4. Document Your Process, Then Make Sure It Is Followed
It’s one thing to have a process in place; it’s another to ensure everyone follows it. I’ve seen how a well-documented process can save a project from veering off course. Without clear documentation, it’s easy for team members to misinterpret expectations. But documentation is only half the battle—you need to make sure everyone is on board and sticking to it.

## 5. Provide Regular Time to Focus
Interruptions are the enemy of productivity. In my experience, giving the team regular, uninterrupted time to focus on their tasks leads to better results. It’s amazing what can be accomplished when developers are given time to work without the constant ping of emails or meeting requests. I’ve found that scheduling blocks of “focus time” is a simple but powerful way to boost productivity.

## 6. Meetings Don’t Write Code
This one feels like an obvious truth, but it’s easy to forget. Too many meetings can seriously derail progress. Yes, meetings are necessary, but it’s important to find the right balance. Every minute spent in a meeting is a minute not spent working on deliverables. Keep meetings short, focused, and, when possible, consider alternatives like quick check-ins or updates via Slack.

## 7. One Deliverable, One Person
Assigning a deliverable to multiple people can create confusion. I’ve found that it’s far better to assign each deliverable to one person who is ultimately responsible for it. This creates clear accountability and avoids situations where tasks fall through the cracks because “someone else was supposed to handle it.”

## 8. A Project Depends on Teamwork
No matter how skilled individuals are, projects succeed because of teamwork. Collaboration, communication, and mutual support are what really drive success. It’s essential to create a work environment where team members can rely on each other, share their knowledge, and work together to solve problems. Strong teamwork keeps a project on track, even when challenges arise.

## 9. Communicating Is Key
I can’t stress this enough: good communication is crucial in every stage of a project. Whether it’s with the team, stakeholders, or users, clear and consistent communication prevents misunderstandings, aligns expectations, and helps resolve issues quickly. A project that suffers from poor communication is often doomed to fail, no matter how well everything else is going.

## 10. Increase Communication: Hold Frequent, Instant Meetings
Following from the previous point, I’ve found that holding quick, frequent meetings can be incredibly helpful. These aren’t hour-long meetings that drag on but brief, to-the-point check-ins where the team can sync up and share quick updates. It helps keep everyone aligned without wasting time. Tools like video calls or even group chats are great for this.

## 11. Developers Hate Status Reports, Managers Love Them
Finally, let’s talk about status reports. In my experience, developers often see status reports as a chore, while managers love them because they provide clear insight into how a project is progressing. It’s important to strike a balance. I’ve found that creating a simple, efficient way to provide status updates helps keep both parties happy. Developers don’t feel bogged down, and managers get the information they need.

---

## Final Thoughts
Project management isn’t easy, but applying these principles has helped me lead successful projects, time and time again. Every project is unique, but focusing on user involvement, clear communication, simplicity, and teamwork goes a long way. I hope these insights are helpful, whether you’re new to project management or looking for ways to improve your current approach.
